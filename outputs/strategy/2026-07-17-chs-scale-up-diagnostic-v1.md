## The business on one page

- Founded 2018 as a branch off Cool Hollow Financial. Original purpose: consult business owners and funnel them into premium finance services to offset tax.
- 24 clients, $60K/month revenue, booming for the last two years.
- Average client retention: about 12 months.
- Team of 6: Mark (face and rainmaker), Cam (CFO), Chad (junior advisor), Cory (junior advisor), Kathy (operations), Hannah (people).
- Niche: blue-collar businesses.
- Acquisition channels: networking events, boardroom, LAN advisory, the Cool Hollow mastermind, referrals. Roughly $200K/year spent across travel and event fees.
- Pricing: was $3,500/month, moving toward $5,000-7,000 with pushback.
- Mark: 7-8 meetings a day, heavy travel, no replacement candidate, needed almost everywhere.
- Team: experienced, picky about hiring, fears the change that comes with growth.
- Relationship loyalty blocks decisions (example: the social media manager underperforms but the relationship matters to Mark, so nothing changes).
- Target: $3-5M/year with at most 2 added heads.

## The math as it stands (rough, on Matt's numbers)

- **Run rate.** $60K/month is $720K/year.
- **The fee gap.** 24 clients at the $3,500 list fee would be $84K/month. Actual is $60K, so the real blended fee is about $2,500 per client per month. Somewhere in the book there are discounts, legacy prices, or lighter scopes. Cam can tell us which, and it matters: the firm is already discounting the old price while trying to raise the new one.
- **The treadmill.** Twelve-month average retention means the client book replaces itself roughly once a year. Standing still requires winning about 24 new clients every year, forever.
- **Acquisition cost.** About $200K/year of travel and event spend against roughly that many new clients is $8,000+ per new client, about 28 cents of every revenue dollar. And it is not bought with money so much as with Mark's calendar and the team's energy.
- **Client value.** A typical client is worth about $30K over their 12 months at the blended fee. Winning one costs $8K+ plus a piece of Mark. The cash ratio survives; the humans are the constraint that does not.
- **The target multiple.** $3M/year is $250K/month, 4.2x today, with at most 8 people. Revenue per person has to roughly triple or quadruple. Hours cannot triple. That leaves exactly four levers: higher prices, one-to-many formats, productized delivery by the junior advisors, and partner-delivered work with a margin. Every recommendation below pulls at least one of them.

## The diagnosis in one paragraph

Cool Hollow Solutions is a relationship business monetized like a time business. Demand is generated by Mark's physical presence at expensive events. Delivery leans on Mark's expertise. The retainer has no defined edge, so deep work leaks in for free, which is exactly why clients push back on $5K: nobody can say precisely what it buys. And the average relationship ends after a year because there is nothing structured to belong to between and after engagements. The result is expensive clients, exhausted people, and capped growth. Mike's estimate that 3-5x revenue is available from existing clients alone fits this picture: the underlying assets (trust, results, reputation, a proven webinar motion) are real and underpriced. The offer architecture is what wastes them.

## The five bottlenecks, ranked

1. **The retainer has no edge.** Undefined scope creates free deep work, burnout, and price resistance at the same time. This is the highest-leverage fix and it is Mike's workstream: retainer becomes guidance-only at the new price, and anything hands-on becomes a separately priced expander with an assigned team. The pricing conversation changes from "why does the same thing cost more" to "here is what is included, and here is the menu."
2. **Acquisition is Mark's body.** Travel plus 7-8 meetings a day is a channel that cannot scale and is burning the one irreplaceable asset. The community (paid, $100-200 entry) plus reviving Mark's webinar motion builds a funnel that works while he sleeps. His first CHS clients came from free-info webinars that drew about 100 people; this is proven, just never monetized.
3. **Twelve-month retention.** In a relationship business this should be 3+ years (Mike's own engagements ran 2-3 years). The fix is structural: a relationship management function that owns dialogue between projects, an exit-reason autopsy so we know why people actually leave, and the ACE ladder so every client always has a next thing (community, mastermind, expander, premium tier). Every 6 months of added average retention is worth about $15K more per client at today's blended fee.
4. **Mark concentration.** Sales, delivery, and brand all live in one person, which is, ironically, the exact disease Business Without You cures for clients. The cure is the same here: productize delivery so Chad and Cory carry standardized products ("delivered by average people at low cost," Mike's phrase), reserve Mark for the premium tier and community authority, and long-term price his personal time at $20K/month for hobby clients (Matt's idea on the call, Mike agreed).
5. **Change resistance and relationship loyalty.** The team fears growth-change, and Mark protects relationships even when they underperform (the social media situation). Do not fight this head-on. The workshops in August and September are the vehicle for consent, and the funnel work routes around the social media problem entirely: nobody has to be fired for a parallel channel to start producing. Results will make the argument that confrontation would lose.

## What good looks like (scenario shape, not a forecast)

Illustrative only, using today's numbers: 30-40 retainer clients at $5K with a clear scope, a paid community of a few hundred members between $100 and $500 a month, expander projects billed on top, events that at least pay for themselves instead of costing $200K, and Mark priced as the scarce premium tier. That shape clears $250K/month without doubling headcount. It only works if retention stretches well past 12 months and delivery comes off Mark's plate, which is why bottlenecks 1 and 3 come first.

## Risks and honest unknowns

- Repricing the existing book can spike churn if handled bluntly. New clients first.

## Open questions (who to ask)

3. **The churn autopsy (Kathy/Cam).** For the last 10 clients who left: finished, drifted, or priced out? Did any come back or refer after leaving?
4. **Mark's week (Mark).** Of the 7-8 daily meetings, what is the split between sales, delivery, and free favors? And which recurring client questions could Chad or Cory answer today if clients would take them?
5. **The pushback source (Mark).** Is the $5-7K resistance coming from existing clients being moved up, or from new prospects? Grandfathering fixes the first; positioning and the challenge-discovery model fix the second.
6. **Honest capacity (whole team).** How many clients can the current 6 serve well, today, with nothing changed? The gap between that number and 24 is the free growth available before anything structural has to change.

Plus two small ones: the exact date of "the 11th" workshop, and what the social media arrangement actually is (what is paid, what was promised, so we know what routing around it costs).
