# 12-Month Business Study Plan

Started: Friday, July 10, 2026. Target finish: July 2027.

**Why this exists:** Matt did not finish a supply chain management degree and is not going back to college. Instead, he is running a 12-month, part-time, self-directed study program at home (books, textbooks, podcasts) to build real business expertise, modeled on the influences behind Alex Hormozi and Michael Ruckman (Senteo). The goal is not trivia. Every anchor book gets applied directly to Cool Hollow Coaching and Business Without You as it's read.

**Companion file:** `2026-07-10-compiled-learning-notes.md` distills the anchor book of each phase. Read the matching section before starting each book.

---

## Week 1: primer

- **The Almanack of Naval Ravikant**: free PDF and audiobook at navalmanack.com. Short, sets the first-principles mental model for the whole year. Read this before anything else.

## Months 1-2: Psychology & Influence

- **Anchor: Influence** by Robert Cialdini. Apply straight to the sales page and VSL copy.
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow** by Daniel Kahneman. Dense, one chapter at a time.
- **Predictably Irrational** by Dan Ariely. Swapped in for the Myers/Twenge Social Psychology textbook (that one is a $150-250 university text, low payoff for the time cost; skip unless a cheap used copy turns up).
- Podcast: *Founders* (David Senra), episodes on Rockefeller, Estée Lauder, Edison.

## Month 2 (parallel): Senteo Foundation

Moved up from later in the year because Mike/Senteo is an active working relationship (July 14 MVP call).

- **The Experience Economy** by B. Joseph Pine II and James H. Gilmore.
- **The Crisis Experience** by Michael Ruckman. Read before the next real conversation with Mike. If it's hard to find in stores, ask Mike directly for a copy; it's his book and that conversation costs nothing.
- *Designing Experiences* (Rossman & Duerden) is a genuine academic textbook, expensive, dense. Treat as a reference to dip into later, not a front-to-back read this year.

## Month 3: Direct-Response Marketing

- **Scientific Advertising** by Claude Hopkins. Public domain (1923), free, read first.
- **The Ultimate Sales Letter** by Dan Kennedy. Cheap, practical.
- **Breakthrough Advertising** by Eugene Schwartz: deferred to Month 8 (see below). It's the hardest and most expensive book on the list (copies often run $60-125, cult classic, low supply).

## Months 4-5: Corporate Strategy

- **Zero to One** by Peter Thiel. Added: the readable, practical version of "spot monopolistic advantages in tiny niches." Read before Porter.
- **Good Strategy Bad Strategy** by Richard Rumelt. Apply to pressure-testing "community not course."
- **Blue Ocean Strategy** by Kim & Mauborgne.
- **Competitive Strategy** by Michael Porter. Genuine MBA textbook, dense; use as reference, not cover-to-cover.
- Podcast: *Acquired*, *Invest Like the Best*.

## Months 6-7: Operations & Scaling

- **The Goal** by Eliyahu Goldratt. Written as a novel, easy read, same Theory of Constraints logic already built into the Bottleneck Breakthrough Plan tool (Milestone 6 of the program).
- **High Output Management** by Andrew Grove. Short, legendary.
- **Working Backwards** by Bryar & Carr. Good, long; reference.
- Podcast: *My First Million*, *How I Built This*.

## Month 8: The deferred read, plus catch-up

- **Breakthrough Advertising** by Eugene Schwartz, deferred from Month 3. By now the side-hustle income should cover it, or a library copy will do.
- This month is also the built-in buffer: finish anything half-read, and write the one-line "what does this change about Business Without You?" note for every book finished so far.

## Month 9: Relationship & Customer Management

- **The Ultimate Question 2.0** by Fred Reichheld. Short, applies directly to the community/retention model.
- **The Relationship Economy** by John DiJulius.
- *CRM at the Speed of Light* by Paul Greenberg: dated, technical. Lower priority; practical CRM gets learned hands-on through the GoHighLevel build anyway.

## Months 10-11: Agility & Change

- **Leading Change** by John Kotter.
- **Competing in the Age of AI** by Iansiti & Lakhani. Directly relevant given the AI-clone content plans.

## Month 12: Capstone

- **Poor Charlie's Almanack** by Charlie Munger. Big, dense, the year's synthesis read, saved for last on purpose.

## Note on "physics-based problem-solving" / Elon Musk framework

No real textbook exists for this specifically. Free substitute: Tim Urban's *Wait But Why* Elon Musk series, plus the first-principles material already covered by Naval and Munger above.

## How to actually get through it part-time

- One **anchor book** per phase: read with a pen, notes taken.
- Everything else on **audiobook via Libby** (free library app, most of this list is on it) during chores or drive time.
- After every anchor book, write one sentence: *what does this change about Business Without You?* That's the difference between a year of trivia and a year of compounding.

## Budget reality

Bought new, the full list runs roughly $300-450. Given the current zero-budget constraint (side hustle targeting the $15K debt first), the free-first path:

- Library card plus the Libby app for ebooks and audiobooks (most titles available free).
- ThriftBooks / AbeBooks for used copies ($4-8 vs $18-25 new) on anything not on Libby.
- Scientific Advertising and The Almanack of Naval Ravikant are free outright, no library needed.
- Breakthrough Advertising and Poor Charlie's Almanack are the two genuinely expensive ones; library first, buy only once the side-hustle income is flowing.
