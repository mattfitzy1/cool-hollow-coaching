# The Independent Consultant's Library

The best general books and tools for consulting to any business, not just Cool Hollow Coaching. The goal: Matt as a professional business consultant in his own right, able to walk into any small or mid-sized company, diagnose it, and get paid for the fix. This also directly serves the side hustle (consulting services, $2-4K/month, debt paid by December).

## Tier 1: The money-makers (read these first, they pay for themselves)

These five are the difference between "guy with opinions" and "consultant who gets hired again." Given the side hustle is live now, these come before anything else on this list.

1. **Flawless Consulting** by Peter Block. The single most respected book on the craft of consulting. Covers the whole engagement arc: contracting (getting clear on what you're actually being hired to do), diagnosis, feedback, and dealing with resistance. Its core insight: most consulting failures are contracting failures. You agreed to the wrong job on day one.
2. **The Trusted Advisor** by David Maister, Charles Green, and Robert Galford. How clients come to trust you, with a formula worth memorizing: trust = (credibility + reliability + intimacy) / self-orientation. The divisor is the killer. The moment a client senses the advice serves your invoice rather than their business, everything above the line dies.
3. **Million Dollar Consulting** by Alan Weiss. The business OF consulting: positioning, packaging, and above all pricing. Weiss's hill to die on: never bill hourly. Price the value of the outcome, not your time. An hourly consultant is punished for being fast and good. His follow-up *Value-Based Fees* goes deeper if pricing becomes your sticking point.
4. **Financial Intelligence** by Karen Berman and Joe Knight. How to actually read a P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow statement, including where the numbers are estimates dressed up as facts. A consultant who can't read financials is guessing. You've already built tools on this ground; this book fills in the formal footing under them.
5. **The Personal MBA** by Josh Kaufman. The whole map of business in one book: value creation, marketing, sales, finance, systems, psychology. Not deep on anything, deliberately, but it means no client conversation lands on territory you've never seen. The best single substitute for the degree you're not going back for.

## Tier 2: The diagnostic toolkit (how to see what's wrong with any business)

6. **The E-Myth Revisited** by Michael Gerber. Why most small businesses are jobs in disguise: a technician who had a heart attack of ambition, now trapped doing the work AND running the shop. The fix is working ON the business, building it like a franchise prototype that runs on systems, not heroics. The most universally applicable diagnosis in small-business consulting.
7. **Traction** by Gino Wickman. The EOS operating system: vision, scorecard, rocks, meeting rhythm, accountability chart. Here's your unfair advantage: Cool Hollow Solutions literally runs on EOS and you're its Head of Operations. You're not learning this from a book, you're living it daily. Read it so you can teach and install what you already practice. This alone is a sellable service (EOS-style operating rhythm for small companies).
8. **Built to Sell** by John Warrillow. A business's value is its ability to run and grow without its owner. Told as a story, fast read. Gives you the "sellability" lens: even owners who never plan to sell get sharper businesses by building as if they would.
9. **Profit First** by Mike Michalowicz. Cash management for real small businesses: take profit first, run the business on what's left, separate accounts doing the discipline the owner won't. Simple, occasionally crude, and it works for exactly the companies most likely to hire you.
10. **Bulletproof Problem Solving** by Charles Conn and Robert McLean. The McKinsey seven-step method by two ex-partners: define the problem precisely, break it into a logic tree, prioritize what matters, analyze, synthesize, tell the story. This is the repeatable engine for walking into any industry cold and finding the answer anyway, which is the actual job of a consultant.

## Tier 3: The professional polish (what separates the top 5%)

11. **The Pyramid Principle** by Barbara Minto. How consultants structure thinking and writing: answer first, then grouped supporting arguments, then evidence. The house style of every top firm. Once you see it, you can't unsee how much rambling costs people. Your reports and pitches level up immediately.
12. **SPIN Selling** by Neil Rackham. The research-backed classic on selling big-ticket intangibles. Sequence of questions (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff) that gets the client to articulate the cost of their problem themselves. Consultative selling is the only kind that works for consulting.
13. **Getting Naked** by Patrick Lencioni. A short fable on winning clients through vulnerability: enter the conversation helping, ask the dumb question, tell the kind truth, never manage your image. The antidote to consultant stiffness, and it pairs perfectly with The Trusted Advisor.
14. **Business Model Generation** by Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur. The Business Model Canvas: any company on one page, nine boxes. As a consultant it's your fastest first-meeting tool: sketch the client's model live, and the broken box usually reveals itself while you're still drawing.

## The universal diagnostic kit (tools, not books)

The frameworks worth carrying into any business, any industry:

- **The Business Model Canvas**: the whole business on one page, first meeting, every time.
- **The three financial statements plus unit economics**: what does one unit of this business (a job, a client, an order) actually earn after direct costs? Most owners don't know. Finding out is billable work.
- **The 13-week cash flow forecast**: you already built this as a tool. It works for any business on earth, not just program clients.
- **Theory of Constraints**: every system has one bottleneck; find it, everything else waits. Also already in your toolkit.
- **The EOS scorecard and meeting rhythm**: 5-15 measurable weekly numbers plus a disciplined weekly meeting. Installable in almost any company, and you've run it for real.
- **NPS with loop-closing**: one question, then actually calling the detractors. Cheap, powerful, underused.
- **Porter's Five Forces**: for when a client's problem isn't internal but structural to their industry. (Covered in the 12-month plan's strategy phase.)
- **SPIN questioning**: works in the sales conversation and doubles as a diagnostic interview with a client's team.

Notice the pattern: your seven milestone tools are already generalized versions of half this kit. The tools you built for Business Without You are also your consulting toolkit. Same engine, wider road.
